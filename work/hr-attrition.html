Where are we losing people, and why do new hires leave?

A fictional Ontario logistics company asked why attrition stayed high. I built a workforce model from hire and exit records and found that the problem sits in the first year of employment, in a few hiring channels, shifts and teams.

Data: synthetic, Harbourline Logistics is fictionalScope: about 1,300 employees, 2023 to 2025Tools: SQL Server, Power BI, DAX, Python

Key findings

    01 · THE PATTERN

    Attrition is a first-year problem

    Company-wide attrition ran at a year. But in 2025, of everyone who left had been with the company for less than a year.

    Across people hired from 2023 to 2025, left within 12 months. Warehouse Operations and Customer Service lost about four in ten.

    Attrition = exits ÷ average month-end headcount. First-year attrition counts only hires with 12 full months of data.

    03 · THE EARLY WARNING

    The 90-day pulse score predicts who will leave

    New hires who answered 1 or 2 to "I would recommend working here" at 90 days left within a year of the time. Those who answered 4 or 5 left of the time.

    A few teams stand out: four warehouse leaders lost more than half of their new hires.

    Recommendations

    Talent acquisition

    Shift hiring spend from agencies to referrals

    Pilot a referral bonus for warehouse roles and compare first-year attrition against agency hires in the same months.

    Measure: first-year attrition by channel
    People leaders

    Act on the 90-day pulse

    Every new hire who scores 1 or 2 gets a check-in with their leader and HR within two weeks.

    Measure: first-year attrition by pulse score
    Operations

    Start with night shift and four teams

    Review shift patterns and onboarding for night-shift hires, beginning with the teams at the top of the leader table.

    Measure: first-year attrition by shift and leader

    Data, model and method

    01 · SOURCEHR recordsHires, exits, exit reasons and 90-day pulse scores (synthetic)
    02 · SQLSQL ServerStaging, star schema with keys and checks, views with business names
    03 · MODELStar schemaEmployee event fact with employee and date dimensions; headcount from hire and exit dates
    04 · MEASUREDAXAverage headcount, attrition, first-year cohort attrition, regretted share
    05 · SECURERow-level securityPeople leader role shows each manager only their own team

    Core measures (from the model)

    Average Headcount =
AVERAGEX (
    VALUES ( 'Date'[Month End] ),
    VAR D = 'Date'[Month End]
    RETURN
        COUNTROWS (
            FILTER ( Employee, Employee[Hire Date] <= D && ( ISBLANK ( Employee[Exit Date] ) || Employee[Exit Date] > D ) )
        )
)

First-Year Attrition =
DIVIDE (
    CALCULATE ( [Hires], Employee[First Year Outcome] = "Left in first year" ),
    CALCULATE ( [Hires], Employee[First Year Outcome] IN { "Left in first year", "Stayed past first year" } )
)

New-Hire Pulse Score =
VAR Hired =
    CALCULATETABLE ( VALUES ( 'Employee Event'[Employee Key] ), 'Employee Event'[Event Type] = "Hire" )
RETURN
    CALCULATE ( AVERAGE ( Employee[90-Day Pulse Score] ), TREATAS ( Hired, Employee[Employee Key] ) )

    Copilot-ready model

    • Model prepared to Microsoft's Copilot guidance; Copilot itself not run.
    • Star schema, business names, a description on every measure and visible column, synonyms in the key measure descriptions, hidden keys and a marked date table.

    Limits of this analysis

    • The data is synthetic: the drivers were planted in a simulation so the report could be tested against a known answer.
    • Findings are correlations, not causes.
    • 2026 is excluded from attrition rates because it is only nine months.
