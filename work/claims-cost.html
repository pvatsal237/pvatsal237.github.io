Where is claims cost going, and what slows settlement?

A fictional Ontario property and casualty insurer wanted to know which claims drive cost and why some take months to settle. I built a claims model in SQL Server and Power BI, a 12-month cost forecast, and a what-if on settling faster.

Key findings

  1. Home water damage is the cost problem: its average cost rose 44% from 2023 to 2026, faster than any other peril.
  2. App-reported claims close in a median of 31 days, against 42 by phone and 54 through brokers.
  3. Litigated claims are only 6% of the total but cost 2.8× as much, and long-open claims are the ones that end up in court.
01 · WHERE COST IS GOING

Water damage is rising fastest

Fire and business interruption are the most expensive claims, but they are stable. Home water damage is the one to watch: its average cost went from $14,478 in 2023 to $20,858 in 2026.

Two weather events, the July 2024 storm and the August 2025 hail, set the monthly peaks. The 12-month forecast leaves them out so it shows the underlying trend: about $47.6M, close to the last 12 months.

Severity = average incurred cost per claim, excluding denied claims. Incurred = indemnity + rental or living expenses + legal cost.

02 · WHAT SLOWS CLAIMS DOWN

Every extra month open adds cost

Claims closed within 30 days cost $8,023 on average. Claims open more than 180 days cost $35,408: rental cars and temporary housing keep running, and litigation becomes likelier.

Within the same team, the busiest adjusters are the slowest, so workload balancing is a lever alongside moving reporting to the app.

These are associations. Complex claims are both slower and costlier; the report separates channel and workload to show where speed can be won.

03 · WHAT FASTER SETTLEMENT SAVES

Ten days faster saves about $680K a year

A what-if parameter in the report lets a manager choose how many days sooner claims settle. The saving is the daily rental or living expense on each claim, times the days saved, up to the days that claim actually paid.

The saving is direct; fewer long-open claims also mean fewer lawsuits, which this estimate does not count.

Recommendations

Claims operations

Push reporting to the app

App claims arrive with photos and details, so they close about ten days sooner than phone claims.

Measure: share of claims reported by app
Team leads

Balance adjuster workloads

Move new claims away from the busiest adjusters within each team, starting with Property.

Measure: median days to close by adjuster
Underwriting

Review water-damage pricing

Severity is rising over 10% a year; check deductibles and prevention discounts before renewal.

Measure: water-damage severity change %

Core measures (from the model)

Estimated Savings =
VAR Faster = [Days Faster Value]
RETURN
    SUMX (
        FILTER ( Claim, Claim[Expense Days] > 0 ),
        Claim[Daily Expense] * MIN ( Faster, Claim[Expense Days] )
    )

Open Claims =
VAR D = MAX ( 'Date'[Date] )
RETURN
    COUNTROWS (
        FILTER (
            CALCULATETABLE ( Claim, REMOVEFILTERS ( 'Date' ) ),
            Claim[Report Date] <= D && ( ISBLANK ( Claim[Close Date] ) || Claim[Close Date] > D )
        )
    )

Severity Change % =
DIVIDE ( [Average Severity] - [Severity Prior Year], [Severity Prior Year] )

Limits of this analysis

  • The data is synthetic: the drivers were planted so the report could be tested against a known answer.
  • Findings are associations, not causes.
  • The forecast is a simple seasonal model; a catastrophe load would sit on top.
